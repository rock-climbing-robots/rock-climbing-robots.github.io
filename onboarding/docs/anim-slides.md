# Animated slides — the kit's guide, for Claude

This file is MANAGED by the `make-anim-slides` skill: `update` overwrites it.
What is specific to this course (its subject, notations, figures) lives in the
project's `CLAUDE.md`, which imports this file.

The slides are not pre-rendered videos: they are animations drawn live on a
`<canvas>`, by a small manim-like engine, inside a reveal.js shell.

## Two rules that are not negotiable

1. **Static site.** The result is served as-is by any file server (Apache,
   `python3 -m http.server`, GitHub Pages): HTML/CSS/JS/SVG/WOFF2 only, no
   server code, no build step. `tools/serve.js` exists only because ES
   modules need HTTP locally; the site does not depend on it.
2. **Everything is computed in the browser, and stays vector.** Every frame
   redraws the scene as strokes and curves: no sprite, no image of a formula,
   no pre-rendered animation. A slide stays sharp at any window size.
   Exceptions: a photograph or illustration (`image()`, `assets/images/`) and
   a film of something that cannot be computed live (`video()`, drawn INTO
   the canvas so that everything the engine draws can go on top of it).
   Before reaching for a film, see "A real robot, drawn live": a machine
   whose model you have is not an exception -- it is computed in the browser
   like everything else, and then it can be posed, turned and taken apart.

Corollary: no npm dependency in the site. The only libraries shipped are in
`vendor/` (reveal.js, MathJax, highlight.js, the fonts).

## How we work

- **The user describes the content of each slide; we add it together. Never
  invent course content.** Proposing an idea is fine — on the review board,
  as a suggestion (below), not in a lecture.
- Talk to the user in the language they write in.
- Code, file names and technical comments are in English. The slides are in
  the language of the course (`lang` in `deck.config.js`).
- Continuity between two scenes: each scene TAKES OVER the picture the
  previous one left (same objects, same positions) and removes it with an
  animation, rather than starting on a different picture. Factor the end
  state of a scene into a function both scenes call.
- **An illustration is a function, not a stretch of a scene.** What a slide
  SHOWS (a plot fitted step by step, an algorithm running, a mechanism) is
  written as `async function name(s, alongside = [])`, called by the scene
  between its own furniture (a title, a frame, a line of prose). That is what
  lets the lecture's text play the same animation alone (see "The text
  mode"), and it keeps the scenes short. Build it this way from the start,
  even for a lecture without a text: one may be asked for later.
- **A lecture with a text (`text: true`) keeps it in step with its slides.**
  A slide added, removed, reordered or changed is, IN THE SAME CHANGE, a
  section of `lectures/text/<NN_slug>.js` written again, and a figure
  checked (see "The text mode"). A proposal on the review board shows both.
- **Readable from the back of the lecture hall, or useless.** Glosses and
  captions stay at 25 and above, column headings around 30, a supporting
  formula around 32, an equation that IS the slide between 46 and 72. When a
  column runs out of room, the FIGURE shrinks, not the font. Text is wide:
  split a long sentence over two lines rather than reducing the size.
- Illustration slides: figure on the left, text column on the right, left
  aligned and vertically centred (`textColumn`).
- Vectors and matrices use brackets (`bmat(...)`: `bmat(2, 1)` is a column
  vector, `bmat([1, 0], [0, 1])` a matrix). Axes: x red, y green, z blue
  (`X_COLOR`, `Y_COLOR`, `Z_COLOR`).

## The look of the pages to read (texts and practicals)

A text (`read.html`) and a practical (`tp_*.html`) are pages to READ, and
they share one look -- keep it when touching `reader.css`, `tp.css`,
`toc.css`:

- **A sheet over the sketch.** The prose (Computer Modern, 21px) sits in a
  46rem column on an opaque sheet 52rem wide, centred; titles are in Lato.
  The sheet's edge is marked by a SHADOW, not by a fade: dark grey on the
  light theme, a pale glow on the dark one. The animated backdrop only shows
  in the margins. On the light theme the sheet is WHITE (`#ffffff`), and the
  figures of a text are drawn on white too, so that they are not grey boxes
  on it; boxes inside the text (callouts, the warning of a text) take a very
  light grey instead. Below 56rem the sheet fills the window, no shadow.
- **The backdrop is the low-poly mesh** (`sketch.js`) behind every text and
  every practical, and nothing else: a course that wants a backdrop of its
  own (drawings of its subject, animated) puts it on the LANDING PAGE only,
  in `lectures/lib/`, imported by `index.html` in place of `sketch.js`.
- **Live figures in a practical**: `<canvas data-figure="name">` in the
  markdown; the page then imports the course's `figures.js` and calls its
  `drawFigures()` (drawn in the palette, redrawn on a theme switch).
- **A bar at the top**, the same on both: "← <course>" back to the landing
  page, where one is (the lecture, or the practical's title), and the theme
  button in its right end; a text adds "Slides ↗". It stays in place as the
  page scrolls, and the outline starts under it.
- **What sits outside the sheet.** The slides of a section (`.tx-go`,
  "slides 12–14") are in the right margin, past the sheet's edge, on a chip
  of their own (back above their section below 72rem). The OUTLINE is in
  the left margin (below).
- **The outline** (`engine/toc.js`, `toc.css`): a small card fixed on the
  left, listing every title of the page (h1, h2, h3, indented by level); a
  click scrolls to it; a title scrolled past (above the bar, or the top of
  the window) is struck through and faded; a long outline scrolls itself so
  the next title stays in view. It is anchored to the SHEET, not to the
  window: its right edge stays 2rem before the sheet whatever the width,
  it narrows only when the window leaves less room, and hides below 80rem.
  `toc.js` imports nothing, so a pandoc page loads it as well as the engine.
- **Sections** of a practical open on a thin rule above their title rather
  than on blank space, and a sub-title right under its title sits close to
  it.
- **Steps** of a practical (`<div class="step">`) are plain prose -- NO box,
  no background. In the left margin: a tick box, and under it the step's
  number, "7/15" (both added by `tp.js`: never number steps by hand).
  Ticking a step throws a few short green sparks out of its box (nothing
  with `prefers-reduced-motion`); a step done fades to 55% and STAYS faded,
  even under the mouse. Where there is no margin, the step makes room for
  its box.
- **Listings** of a practical are DARK in both themes (github-dark), like
  the terminal or the editor the code goes into -- the light theme does not
  turn them over.

## The engine (`engine/`)

Modelled on manim, without manim. A "mobject" owns its geometry in WORLD
coordinates; moving it means moving its points.

| `mathjax.js`, `svgutil.js` | LaTeX → `Path2D` outlines |

### Coordinates and sizes

**Exactly** manim's: a 14.22 × 8 slide, x to the right, **y up**, origin at
the centre; `strokeWidth: 4` = 0.04 unit; `size: 30` for text = manim's
`font_size=30`. The two calibration constants of `text.js` (height of "Hxg"
and "x" at size 48) are measured in the browser at startup: never "fix" them
by hand.

### Writing a scene

```js
import { scene, slideTitle, chapterTitle, text, math, textColumn,
         Arrow, FadeIn, Write, appear, to, DOWN } from "../engine/index.js";

export const title = "Vectors";
export const icons = ["settings.svg"];   // preloaded (makeExercise needs this one)

const Intro = scene("Intro", async (s) => {
  s.add(slideTitle("A vector"));
  const u = new Arrow([-4, -1], [-1, 1.5]);
  const caption = text("an arrow with a direction and a length", { size: 30 });
  textColumn([caption]);
  await s.play(appear(u), new Write(caption));
  await s.nextSlide();                        // end of the slide
  await s.play(to(u, (m) => m.shift(DOWN)));  // manim's .animate
  await s.nextSlide();
});

export const scenes = [Intro];
```

- `await s.play(anim, anim, { runTime, rateFunc })`: animated mobjects enter
  the scene by themselves, as in manim.
- `await s.nextSlide()` ends the slide. `{ loop: true }` makes the slide that
  STARTS loop forever; `{ autoNext: true }` moves on by itself.
- `to(mob, (m) => ...)` is `.animate`; the callback must RETURN the mobject.
- `redraw(() => ...)` is `always_redraw`: rebuilt every frame. It can be
  faded out directly.
- `new Value(0)` is the `ValueTracker`; `valueTo(t, 8)` / `valueBy(t, TAU)`
  animate it, and an updater or a `redraw` makes the figure follow.
- A lecture module exports `title`, `scenes`, and optionally `icons` and
  `images` (preloaded before the first slide).

### Making things appear: `appear`

Nothing should just fade in. `appear(mob)` picks the manim gesture that suits
what it is given, and it is the ONLY place that knows the mapping:

| given | plays |
| --- | --- |
| a straight arrow | `GrowArrow`: it grows from its tail |
| a curve, circle, rectangle | `Create`: the stroke is drawn |
| a `Dot` | `Create`: its radius swells from the centre |
| text, a formula | `Write`: glyph by glyph (a fade with `write: false` in the config) |
| a group | all of that, members staggered (`lag`) |

Two `play`s in a row IN THE SAME SLIDE (arrows first, then what they point
at) lead the eye instead of posing a finished picture. `mob.appearAs =
"fade"` forces a fade (a fill-only shape has no stroke to draw and would pop
in on the last frame).

### Instant navigation, and what it imposes

There is no video to rewind: to show slide *k*, the player **replays the
scene from its start** in instant mode (every animation jumps to `alpha = 1`,
nothing is drawn), then plays slide *k* for real. Any slide can be reached,
forwards or backwards, at once. Consequences:

- `construct` must be **deterministic** and free of side effects: no mutated
  global state, no unseeded randomness. **Every random draw goes through
  `seeded(seed)`**, and is computed ONCE at module load, not inside
  `construct`.
- An animation must be correct when run in one go: never rely on going
  through the frames one by one.
- A heavy computation is redone at every jump: keep it small, or precompute
  it at module load.
- **A looping slide must be PERIODIC.** It replays the SAME animation
  objects, and `begin()` re-anchors them on the current state: use
  `valueBy(t, TAU)`, `thereAndBack`, or poses that close on the first. A
  lone `valueTo(v, X)` plays once and then never moves again — silently: the
  slide looks right, it is just still. To check, capture several frames
  DURING the loop and compare them (`tools/loops.js`).
- A looping slide is armed by the NEXT `nextSlide()`: after the loop's
  `play()`, write `await s.nextSlide()` (it adds no slide, the block being
  empty). Without it the animation plays once, and whatever follows is
  swallowed into the looping slide. `poseExercise` does it for you.

### Exercises

`makeExercise(lines)` returns `[card, gear]`: a gold banner with a gear that
turns while the students work, `makeHint(mobs)` a grey one. `poseExercise(s,
card, gear)` shows it and loops the gear. The slide BEFORE the banner must end
with `nextSlide({ autoNext: true })`, or an empty slide sneaks in before the
gear turns. The words ("Exercise", "Hint") come from `words` in the config.

### Themes

The light / dark choice holds for the WHOLE site, slides included.
`theme-toggle.js` writes `data-theme` on `<html>` and remembers it;
`engine/palette.js` reads it ONCE at load. A lecture page has no button (its
slide fills the window): it asks for the theme alone with
`<html data-theme-button="off">`, and `ttt` toggles. `?theme=light` forces one
page without touching the remembered choice.

- Never write `"#FFFFFF"` or `"#000000"`: the defaults of shapes, text and 3D
  come from the palette, and the named colours (`FG`, `MUTED`, `ACCENT`...)
  switch by themselves.
- An illustration in WHITE LINES on nothing vanishes on a light background:
  `image(name, { lineArt: true })` draws it inverted. A shaded or coloured
  image must NOT be marked.
- A mobject keeps the colour it was built with; changing the theme reloads
  the page.

### Fonts

Body text AND maths are in **Computer Modern** (CMU Serif for prose, MathJax's
vector fonts for formulas), so that a formula in a sentence belongs to the
sentence. **Titles** (`slideTitle`, `chapterTitle`, section splashes) are in
**Lato medium**: they are the slideshow's furniture, not prose. `?font=lato`
on the URL gives manim's original look.

### Several languages

A lecture is written in ONE language (`lang`) and stays so in its source.
Each other language in `translations` is a dictionary,
`lectures/i18n/<code>.js`, keyed by the string EXACTLY as the lecture hands
it to `text()`, `tex()` or `math()`:

```js
export const DICT = { "Dynamic programming": "Programmation dynamique" };
export const TOKENS = [["play", "jouer"]];   // words inside formulas
```

A missing string is drawn as written, so a lecture not yet translated simply
stays in the source language. A large dictionary can be split per lecture
(`fr_01.js` spread into `fr.js`). `make strings LECTURE=01_x.html LANG=fr`
lists what is still missing. `?lang=fr` forces one page; `ddd` cycles and
remembers. The landing page and the practical pages carry a language button.

- A formula whose text changes with the language: translate the WHOLE source
  string (a `DICT` entry), or its words via `TOKENS`.
- Strings built at runtime (`\`${n} steps\``) cannot be keyed: build them
  from translated pieces (`tr("steps")`).

## Recipes

### A number that changes

A number that changes cannot be typeset by MathJax: its cache is keyed by
source, and a value moving every frame would typeset hundreds of formulas.

- A number alone: `redraw(() => text(fmt(v.get()), { font: "cm" }))`, built
  already in place and pinned by an edge (`alignPointTo(LEFT, anchor)`), or
  it dances as its width changes.
- A number INSIDE a formula: typeset the formula ONCE with a template in each
  slot (`\class{P0}{-0.00}`), hide the template (`part(i).setOpacity(0)`) and
  paint the current value in the box it leaves. The template must be a REAL
  number: formula parts are cut on glyph ink, and a `\phantom` has no box.

### Formula parts

`math(["p = ", "R(q_1)"])` then `eq.part(1)`: parts go through
`\class{P0}{...}` and are typeset in ONE go, so LaTeX spacing stays right.
`TransformMatching` morphs one formula into another, part by part.

### An interactive slide

Two controls, one idea: they are real HTML in the overlay above reveal
(engine/overlay.js), positioned every frame from the mobject, and **their
value lives in the overlay, NOT in the scene** -- the player replays
`construct` on every jump and a value kept in the scene would be reset.
Read it in a `redraw`, or pass a callback. Everything on screen can depend
on it.

- `slider(key, { min, max, step, value, width, reset, onInput })` for a
  NUMBER: an `<input type="range">`; read `sl.value`.
- `handle(key, { at, bounds, label, rad, color, step, reset, onMove })` for
  a POINT one drags: a ring the mouse, the finger and the arrow keys can
  move (`step` is one arrow key); read `h.pos`. It draws nothing itself --
  the dot, the arrow head or the label at that point is drawn by the
  lecture, in a `redraw` that reads `h.pos`, in the colour the ring wears.
  `label` is what a screen reader announces: give it one, the key is a poor
  name for a person. **`bounds` says where it may go**: a rectangle
  `[x0, y0, x1, y1]`, or a FUNCTION from the point the hand asks for to the
  point it settles on -- that is how a point stays on a circle or on a line,
  and it is the right place for every constraint, since `onMove` is told the
  point only once everything else has it.

Both stay where they were left. `reset: true` puts one back where the
lecture set it every time the slide is entered, which suits a scene of ONE
slide; a slide that resets one of its controls resets them ALL, or it comes
back half fresh and half as it was.

A figure whose point one may move says so: a line of prose under it
("drag P"), or the reader believes the picture is fixed. A number that
follows the drag is written with the recipe above (a `redraw` for a number
alone, a template slot in a formula), never typeset again per frame.

### A real robot, drawn live

A machine with meshes -- an industrial arm, a quadruped, a five-bar -- is
drawn by WebGL from its model, not filmed. `tools/robot_webgl.py` writes the
robot as DATA once:

    python3 tools/robot_webgl.py path/to/robot.urdf assets/robots/arm3d \
        [--track motion.json] [--site effector=closing_effector_1]

It needs MuJoCo (`pip install mujoco`) and writes `arm3d.json` (the kinematic
tree, the visual geoms and their colours, where each mesh sits in the binary,
a motion as joint configurations, and a camera framed on that motion) and
`arm3d.bin` (the meshes: float32 positions then uint32 triangles). Without
`--track` the motion is a sweep that takes every joint through its range and
closes on itself. `--site` keeps a frame that MuJoCo welds away (a fixed
joint's child) under a name of its own: that is how a CLOSED chain names the
point its two branches share.

In a lecture:

```js
const data = await loadRobot("arm");            // once, before laying out
const arm = robot3d(data, { width: 11, at: [3.3, -0.2], play: true });
s.add(arm);
const elbow = () => arm.middle("link_3");       // where the eye sees that link
```

- The robot is a mobject in a box of the picture's aspect. `play: true` lets
  the motion run on its own, as a film did; `hold()` stops it where it is and
  `run()` starts it again; `t` is an instant one can animate; `pose` is a
  configuration set outright, for a slide that shows what ONE joint does.
- **Everything drawn over it asks the robot where things are**, in SLIDE
  coordinates: `at(body)` (a body's origin, which sits on its joint),
  `middle(body)` (the centre of what that body SHOWS), `axes(body)` (the tips
  of its three axes, so a frame drawn on it turns with it), `joint(name)`
  (the anchor and the tip of its axis), `point3(p)` (any world point).
  A lecture never touches the 3D itself.
- The camera is `cam` (MuJoCo's: `lookat`, `distance`, `azimuth`,
  `elevation`), a plain field: moving it is an ordinary animation, and
  `cameraBetween` eases from one to another.
- **To step the machine back and leave one link in front**, name the link:
  `robot3d(data, { solid: ["link_3"], dim: 0.16 })`. The whole robot is drawn
  solid and the PICTURE is faded, then those bodies are drawn again at full
  strength (colour for them, depth for all, so what passes in front still
  hides them). Do not make the meshes see-through instead: the machine then
  turns to glass, its own back faces showing through it.
- `paint(bodyIndex)` gives a body a colour of its own, for a lecture that
  names its parts by colour.

The robot is drawn into an offscreen canvas and handed to the engine's canvas
with `drawImage`, so it takes its place in the z-order: everything the course
draws goes ON TOP of it, in strokes, through the same camera.

### Code listings

`code(src, { language: "python" })` is not drawn on the canvas: it is an HTML
element in `#html-layer` (above the canvas AND reveal, so the text can be
selected), coloured by highlight.js, positioned every frame. It follows fades
and moves, not rotations.
**Code blocks: no "Mac" buttons, black background.** A listing is a plain
box, NO title bar with the three coloured buttons, and it is dark (github-dark
on black) in BOTH themes, light slides included. That is the default of
`code()` and of `site.css`: do not pass `bar: true`, and do not restyle a
listing light, unless the user asks for it.

## The text mode (`read.html`)

A lecture may have a TEXT: the same lecture, in the same order, written to
be READ, on `read.html?l=<NN_slug>`. It is a lecture in prose that stands
on its own -- NOT the slides pasted into a page: its figures are the
illustrations of the slides alone (no slide title, no frame, no line of
prose drawn under them: the text says it), and the slides are one button
away, and the text one button away from them.

**Whether a lecture has one is the user's choice**: ask when a lecture is
created (`deck ... --text`), and add one later with `install.mjs text .
<NN_slug>`. Either way the lecture gets `text: true` in
`lectures/manifest.js`: a "Text" link on its card of the landing page, a
"Text" word inside the slide number of its slides (`xxx` too), which open
the text at the section of the slide.

### The file

`lectures/text/<NN_slug>.js` is DATA:

```js
export const sections = [
  { at: ["Branches of machine learning", 1],   // a scene's NAME, a slide's rank in it (0 = first)
    title: { en: "Supervised learning", fr: "L'apprentissage supervisé" },
    level: 3,                                   // h3; h2 without it; no title at all is fine
    body: [
      { en: `prose, <b>HTML</b>, \\(y = f(x)\\)`, fr: `...` },
      { figure: "supervised", caption: { en: "...", fr: "..." }, width: 0.8 },
      { image: "media/go.jpeg", caption: { ... } },
      { video: "media/lace.mp4", caption: { ... } },
    ] },
];
```

- Every string is given in EACH language of the course (`lang` and
  `translations`); the page follows the language of the site. The title's
  anchor (its permalink, `#supervised-learning`) is made from the SOURCE
  language, so a link is the same in every language.
- A section is anchored by a scene's name and the rank of a slide in it,
  never by a slide number, and covers every slide up to the next section's
  anchor: that is how a slide finds its text, and the text its slide (the
  right margin of each section links to its slides; "Slides" at the top opens
  the section being read). A section anchored on a slide that no longer
  exists makes `read.html` THROW: the error names it.
- Formulas go between `\\(` and `\\)` (`\\[ \\]` displayed), with the colour
  names of the slides. Not `$...$`: the strings are template literals, where
  `${` is taken.
- Figures, photographs and videos are numbered ("Figure n.") in order; a
  caption says what the figure shows in one or two sentences.
- `width: 0.75` shrinks a figure whose lettering (sized for the back of the
  hall) would dwarf the prose.
- At the top, a notice says the text was produced automatically from the
  slides, and that the slides are the reference (`words.generated`). It stays
  until the author asks to remove it (`textWarning: false`).

### Writing it

- It explains what the slides SHOW, in full sentences, in the order of the
  slides: a paragraph gives the reasoning a slide leaves to the speaker. It
  adds no course content (no example, fact or claim that is not on the
  slides or in the notes the lecture follows).
- Headings follow the lecture's structure (a scene or a group of slides),
  not one per slide. A slide of pure transition (a box that closes) needs no
  text of its own: the section before covers it.
- A figure goes where the prose needs it, after the paragraph that
  introduces it.

### Figures: the illustrations, factored out

The lecture declares the figures of its text:

```js
export const figures = [
  figure("supervised", [-6.6, -2.75, 6.75, 1.95], async (s) => { await supervisedFit(s); }),
  figure("k-means", [-5.2, -2.9, 5.4, 2.0], async (s) => {
    const dots = await unlabelledDots(s);
    await s.wait(0.6);
    await kMeans(s, dots);
  }),
  figure("agent-environment", [-5.6, -3.4, 6.5, 2.05], agentLoop),
];
```

A figure is a small scene that plays ONE illustration alone, in the part
`[x0, y0, x1, y1]` of the world (the renderer's `view`), on its own canvas
in the page. It MUST be built from the same functions as the slide
(`supervisedFit`, `kMeans`, `agentLoop` above: see "How we work"), never
from a copy: the slide and the figure then cannot drift apart. The slide
calls the function between its furniture, passing its own animations along
(`await supervisedFit(s, [new FadeIn(caption)])`); the figure calls it bare.

In the page, a figure draws how it ENDS (`Player.still`), when it comes near
the screen; ▶ (or a click) plays it live, its `nextSlide()` steps one after
the other; a looping step goes on until stopped. One figure plays at a time,
and one leaving the screen stops.

Choosing the view: the bounding box of the illustration's ink, labels
included, plus a margin of about 0.3. Check it on a screenshot.

Each canvas has its own HTML layer (`Renderer.layer`, engine/overlay.js): a
code listing or a slider is one element PER layer (`entry.at(r)`), so a
figure can hold them too.

### Keeping it up to date

The text is part of the lecture. Every change to the slides of a lecture
with a text comes with its text, in the same change:
- a slide added: the section it belongs to says what it shows (or a new
  section anchored on it); a slide removed: its words go, and an anchor on it
  moves;
- a scene renamed: every `at` naming it;
- an illustration changed: its figure's view checked on a screenshot (the
  function is shared, so the animation follows by itself);
- a new illustration worth a figure: factored into a function, declared in
  `figures`, placed in the text.
Then `read.html?l=<NN_slug>` must load without error (the anchors are
checked there), and a screenshot of the sections touched joins the slides'
ones on the review board.

A request sent with `!!!` from the text has `page: read.html?l=<NN_slug>`:
it is about the TEXT (or a figure); its screenshots are taken on
`read.html?l=<NN_slug>#/<index>` (which opens the section), not with
`tools/shots.js`, which only knows the slides.

## Developing and checking

- `make serve`: the slides on 9090 and the review board on 7777; Ctrl-C stops
  both (`make serve PORT=8123 REVIEW=8124` for other ports). Before starting
  them, check what answers on the port: another course may use the same ones
  (`curl -s localhost:9090/deck.config.js` must show THIS course's `id`).
- A lecture exposes `window.__deck = { player, deck }`: `__deck.deck.slide(12)`
  jumps to a slide, `__deck.player.scene.mobjects` is the current scene.
- Checking without a screen: `npm install puppeteer-core` (ignored by git),
  then `node tools/shots.js <url> <dir> [indices]` (and `BROWSER=firefox` for
  the other engine), and a contact sheet with `montage <dir>/*.png -tile 5x
  -geometry +2+2 -resize 480x270 sheet.png`. `shots.js` names files by slide
  INDEX (0-based); the player DISPLAYS 1-based numbers.
- `tools/drag.js <url> <slide|selector> [--reset]`: takes hold of every
  `handle()` on a slide (or on a figure of a text) and says whether the
  picture followed -- the one thing a screenshot cannot tell you about a
  point one moves. Run it in both browsers, like the shots.
- `tools/overflow.js <url>`: text running off the slide. `tools/layout.js`:
  texts overlapping or grazing each other. Neither replaces looking at the
  contact sheet.
- `tools/slidemap.js <url>`: slide index → scene name. `tools/loops.js`:
  frames captured during looping slides (are they moving?).
  `tools/transition.js`: frames across a scene change (continuity).
  `tools/annotate.js`: circles and arrows on a screenshot, for the board.
- Scene errors show in the browser console: a `construct` that throws leaves
  the page on "…" (indexing the slides fails).

## The review board (`tools/review/`, port 7777)

Where the user and Claude meet. Everything lives in `.review/proposals.json`
(neither versioned nor published). The board is organised in THREADS — a
request or a suggestion, then each version of the change, comments, verdicts —
and its tabs are the stages:

- **User requests**: Claude's turn. A request typed during a presentation
  (`!!!` on a slide), or with the board's **New request** button (on the
  course in general, no slide), a comment on a change, a suggestion the user
  said to do, a rollback to check.
- **Suggestions**: Claude's pedagogical ideas, waiting for "Do it" or
  "Dismiss".
- **To review**: the user's turn: a change with its before/after shots, to
  Accept, Rollback, or comment.
- **Done**.

Claude's side:

1. `node tools/review/queue.js` lists what is waiting for Claude.
2. For a change: find the slide (the `scene` field survives inserted slides;
   the number does not). **Before touching the file**, keep the BEFORE: the
   screenshot (`tools/shots.js <url> <dir> <index>`) and a copy of the file
   under `.review/snap/`. Edit, then take the AFTER at the same index (check
   it: an added `nextSlide()` shifts everything after it).
3. File it, with the thread it answers as `parent`:

```sh
node tools/review/propose.js <<'EOF'
{ "where": "02 · slide 14", "title": "...", "why": "...",
  "before": "/path/before.png", "after": "/path/after.png",
  "file": "lectures/02_vectors.js", "snap": ".review/snap/02-r012.js",
  "page": "02_vectors.html", "slide": 14, "parent": "r012" }
EOF
```

   The change is applied as soon as it is filed; the user accepts it or rolls
   it back. **Never mark a request done by hand**: filing the change with its
   parent does it.
4. **Rollback** is applied ON DISK by the board (`patch -R`, dry run first; a
   failed one changes nothing and says so). The thread then comes back to
   Claude: check the deck still works (shots of the affected slides — another
   change may have leaned on the one undone), then close it with
   `node tools/review/queue.js --rollback-ok <id>`.
5. A pedagogical idea goes in as a suggestion, never straight into a lecture:
   `node tools/review/idea.js` (fields `lecture`, `title`, `why`, `what`,
   `severity` 1–5, optional `shot` from `tools/annotate.js` and `parent`).
6. `node tools/review/watch.js` prints what changes on the board, so that a
   verdict or a request can wake Claude up — see "Watching the board".

Screenshots go in a scratch directory outside the repository (`propose.js`
copies them into `.review/shots/`), never into the site's tree.

### Watching the board

A watch that misses a request is worse than none: the user believes someone
is on it. It happened once — a request sent BEFORE the watch started was
never announced, because the first pass took the board "as it stands".
`watch.js` now announces, at start-up, every thread already waiting for
Claude; the procedure matters as much as the script:

1. **First `node tools/review/queue.js`**, and deal with what is waiting. Do
   not say "I'm watching" before.
2. **Arm the watch**: a Monitor on `node tools/review/watch.js`, with the
   longest timeout allowed. A `WAITING [...]` line is Claude's turn (a
   request, a comment, a suggestion to do, a rollback to check); a
   `verdict [...]` line is a decision of the user's, nothing to do.
   "watching: nothing waiting for Claude" at start-up says all is well.
   "Claude's turn" is computed by `threads.js`, like the board and
   `queue.js`: the three cannot disagree.
3. **Re-arm on every expiry**, without being asked: the start-up pass
   announces again whatever is waiting, so nothing sent between two watches
   is lost.
4. **On a `WAITING`**: `queue.js`, then the steps above (shots, snap, edit,
   `propose.js` with its `parent`).
5. The watch dies with the session. When a session resumes, or the user
   asks whether Claude is watching, check that a Monitor really runs;
   otherwise start again at step 1. Never claim a watch that is not armed.
6. The deck's `!!!` posts to whatever board holds its port: another course
   served on the same port sends its requests here. A request whose `page`
   is not a page of THIS course is pointed out to the user, not handled.

**"start"**: when the user says so, bring the workshop up without asking: the
two servers (`make serve` in the background, after checking the ports) and
the watch described just above — each request answered with a PROPOSAL,
nothing marked done by hand, nothing committed or pushed unless the user
asks.

## The student roulette (if enabled)

`rrr` draws a student with suspense (sending them to the board), `iii` in a
brief spin (a quick question; `↓` spins again). A real wheel: names on a CSS
3D cylinder, the cylinder turns; the winner is chosen FIRST and the wheel
tells the story. A name drawn is out of the hat until everyone has been
called, or the page is reloaded.

The names come from the class list at the root (`etudiants.csv` by default):
the registrar's export as is — semicolons, header lines, the name in the
first column. **That file holds personal data (names, often dates of birth
and addresses, sometimes of minors). It is in `.gitignore` AND excluded from
publishing. Never remove it from either list, never commit it, never open it
into the conversation, never copy it anywhere.** Test the roulette with a
FAKE list. The shortcut fetches the list only on first use: without it a 404
leaves it silent, which is why the code can stay on the public site.

## Publishing ("sync the live")

The site is static: publishing is copying. `PUBLISH` is set in
`Makefile.local` (`host:www/course/`).

1. Check first (`tools/shots.js`): what is sent is online at once.
2. **`make publish-dry`, and READ the list.** `--exclude '.*'` keeps out
   everything hidden at once (`.git`, `.review` — the board, its screenshots
   and comments — `.anim-slides.json`, the `.swp` an editor leaves beside the
   class list, which contain it); `*etudiants*` and `$(ROSTER)` keep the list
   out under any name. A pattern that names one file does not protect its
   neighbours. Anything else the course must never publish (exam questions,
   drafts) goes in `EXTRA_EXCLUDES` in `Makefile.local`, e.g.
   `EXTRA_EXCLUDES := --exclude 'qcm'`: it is added to the list above, and the
   dry run is what proves it.
3. `make publish`. **No `--delete`**: the destination may hold other things.

## Known pitfalls

- **MathJax**: use `tex-svg-full.js` (the normal build fetches its extensions
  over HTTP on first use: 404 offline).
- MathJax has no `\definecolor` (it gives a red `merror`). It lets an unknown
  colour name through, so `engine/mathjax.js` rewrites `\color{accgreen}`,
  `accblue`, `accred`, `accgold`, `accviolet`, `accorange` into the palette's
  colours before typesetting.
- MathJax has **no accented letters**: an "é" in `\text{}` is DROPPED without
  error. `mathjax.js` rewrites accents into TeX commands (`\'e`) — except the
  cedilla. For prose, prefer `tex()`, which sets it in the body font.
- Stretchy delimiters (`\overrightarrow`, `\underbrace`, big brackets) are
  drawn in NESTED `<svg>`s: `getCTM()` stops at the nearest viewport,
  `getScreenCTM()` differs between Chrome and Firefox for the nested `<svg>`
  itself, and the repeated piece overflows on purpose and is clipped. All of
  it is handled in `svgutil.js` (`matrixTo`, `clipOf`). **Always check
  formulas in BOTH browsers** (`BROWSER=firefox node tools/shots.js ...`).
- A brace is a LaTeX brace (`\overbrace` over an empty box of the right
  width): hand-drawn, it would not match the maths. Same principle
  everywhere: prefer LaTeX to a hand-drawn approximation when the stroke must
  match the font.
- Never give a field the name of a `Mobject` method: a `Rectangle`'s corner
  radius is `rad`, because `corner(dir)` exists.
- Order inside `play()`: `begin()` first, THEN the scene hooks (`onBegin`).
- What must LEAVE the scene at the end of an animation is collected BEFORE
  `finish()`: `finish()` restores the mobject from a copy and replaces its
  children.
- **An updater only runs on a mobject the SCENE holds.** A group carrying an
  updater that nobody added stays still; animating its members one by one
  adds the MEMBERS: `s.add(group)` explicitly.
- A parent's opacity MULTIPLIES its children's when drawn: `setOpacity` sets
  it on the targeted node only.
- A group's opacity only shows if the scene draws THE GROUP: the player adds
  each animation's mobject to the scene, and `Scene.add` puts the group in
  place of its first member so the drawing order does not change. Adding a
  group removes its members added alone (and conversely); `scene.remove(x)`
  also detaches `x` from groups that stay on screen.
- `Write` redraws the WHOLE line, clipped glyph by glyph: drawing characters
  one by one loses the kerning.
- A fresh `Text` is centred on the origin, and `arrange()` re-centres the
  group: manim's behaviour, which all layout relies on.
- Text is drawn in screen space at a real pixel font size; `calibrate()` runs
  AFTER `document.fonts.ready` and MathJax's startup, and measures EVERY font.
- `copy()` keeps aliases: a named child and its entry in `children` must stay
  the SAME object.
- A mobject built by `redraw(() => ...)` captures its points in the closure:
  `shift`/`scale` have NO effect on it. Build it at the right place and size,
  or make the closure depend on a tracker.
- A mobject that owns its geometry (fields rebuilt by a `refresh()`) must
  implement `mapPoints` and `onScale`, or every frame brings it back where it
  was built.
- Tabler icons contain a ghost path `<path d="M0 0h24v24H0z"/>` that becomes
  a visible square: remove it (`sed -i '/M0 0h24v24H0z/d' file.svg`). Icons:
  `https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@tabler/icons@latest/icons/outline/<name>.svg`
  (MIT) — downloaded into `assets/icons/`, never hotlinked.
- A video copied while it is being written is TRUNCATED silently: it plays,
  and loops early. Check a copy is complete before committing it.
- Editing files with `str.replace()` without checking: a pattern that no
  longer matches fails SILENTLY. Always assert the replacement happened.
- In `pkill -f`, never use a pattern that also appears in the current shell
  command (it would kill itself).
